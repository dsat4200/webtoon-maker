# Raster brush implementation and fidelity limits

The new renderer is an independent implementation of the researched brush feature families. Its mathematical output has **not** yet been matched against native CSP stroke exports. Public manual descriptions establish the meaning of controls, but do not specify the numerical algorithms. Passing our tests is not evidence of CSP pixel parity.

## Implemented raster behavior

- Circle, flattened and square tips, plus embedded PNG tips with preserved registration padding. Mask, main/sub-color, and original-RGB material modes are distinct. Rotated and flipped image sampling preserves premultiplied alpha at edges. Medium/strong anti-aliasing uses reduced-resolution material images when drawing small tips from large source assets.
- Distance-scheduled dabs, per-tip density, and a separate accumulated stroke-opacity ceiling. Same-stroke intersections stay within the stroke-opacity limit; a subsequent stroke can build on the previous one.
- Spray particles have their own angle and can remain independent, follow the line, follow the resolved whole-brush angle, or face the spray center. Additive angle randomness works with each mode. The first line-oriented particle waits for a movement direction. Earlier saved presets with a fixed particle mode migrate to whole-spray orientation, retaining their previous behavior; legacy outward/random modes remain available.
- World-aligned texture with scale, rotation, brightness, contrast, inversion and density. Per-dab texture changes buildup; per-stroke texture is applied to the completed coverage. Dynamic texture strength tracks the strongest contributing dab at each pixel.
- A secondary stroke plane for dual brushes, with its own scheduler and material settings. Alpha combination and RGB combination are separate options.
- Bilinear ribbon strips with joins that follow the path, pressure-varying width, rotated material aspect ratio, and tip repetition along arc length. This is actual image deformation, not a row of individually rotated stamps. Noncardinal imported ribbon angles remain unresolved: rotated bounding-box corners introduce visible gaps in the downloaded wool and knitting brushes.
- Brush blending modes including destination-out erasure. Active raster-selection coverage is applied to the fully composited result against the original stroke baseline, preventing repeated flushes from progressively expanding antialiased selection edges.
- Wet pickup/transport, paint loading, and watercolor boundaries. These are the independent approximations described below.
- Wet Blend/Running keep picked-up canvas alpha when fresh paint density is zero. Pure blenders with both paint controls zero can transport existing pigment; empty canvas, zero-opacity strokes and transparent tip coverage remain unchanged. Paint density reduces only the fresh alpha contribution.
- Normalized wet-paint amount and density are clamped after dynamics, so a tilt response above 100% saturates the control instead of extrapolating colors or alpha. Stored watercolor-edge blur is dormant during live edge processing and applies only with Process after brush stroke enabled, including its repaint bounds.
- Density, opacity, size, thickness, texture density, hardness, paint amount/density, blur, and color-stretch values supplied by the scheduler are consumed by the raster stage. Tablet sensors and their mappings still depend on the input/scheduler path.
- Signed hue, saturation and brightness offsets are distinct from random variation. Main/sub/both targeting changes the selected colors before their configured blend. Grayscale material regions retain their separate main/sub roles; authored RGB tips receive main/both HSV changes and remain unchanged for sub-only targeting. Sub-color blending uses Standard RGB interpolation or the same declared linear-light approximation as Perceptual wet mixing.
- Post-correction retains the live stabilized path and redraws it at pen-up using an independent distance-based smoother. Endpoints, pressure, tilt, rotation, and timestamps are preserved. Percentage start/end effects and ending effects on spacing resolve against the completed corrected path length, including secondary-brush taper. The replay restores the pre-stroke pixels first, uses the same random seed, and publishes the union of old/new bounds without leaving the live path behind. Fade keeps its distance-from-start behavior when replaying.
- Length-based ending effects show a provisional path immediately and apply the final ending at pen-up; short strokes no longer remain invisible while their entire length is inside the pending ending region. For this preview alone, raw inputs replay through the original independent primary/secondary stabilization, preserving the prior final pixels. Existing post-correction/percentage/spacing replay retains its shared primary-stabilized path behavior.

## Numerical behavior requiring CSP comparison

| Area | Current independent behavior | What remains to establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardness/anti-aliasing | Smooth radial falloff and distinct edge widths; material minification uses smooth image reductions. | CSP falloff curves, size normalization and exact filter kernels. |
| Texture | Ten imported modes follow the corroborated menu order, including Color burn; Normal currently shares Multiply and reports that approximation. | CSP transfer functions, density emphasis, origin conventions, and dynamic interaction. |
| Stroke opacity | Per-pixel maximum dab opacity scales accumulated flow coverage. | CSP behavior when pressure opacity changes within a self-intersecting stroke. |
| Spray orientation | Independent particle offset plus one direction source and a seeded uniform random offset over up to a full turn. | Native particle-angle flag values and random-strength scaling still need controlled exports; spray distribution, thickness and whole-spray geometry remain independent approximations. |
| Color variation | Hue wraps in HSV turns; saturation and brightness/value clamp to their normalized range. Main/sub targeting is applied before blending. | Exact CSP response scaling, distributions, perceptual color-space math, and authored RGB material interaction with sub-color blending. The latter is not applied and receives an import warning when active. |
| Wet Blend | Samples pre-stroke raster pigment into a 64 × 64 brush-local reservoir. Pigment persists over distance and mixes with the configured paint amount/density. | CSP reservoir/pickup and replenishment formulas. Current retention length is independently defined as eight brush diameters times Color stretch. |
| Running color/Smear | Uses retained pigment history with pre-stroke local pickup, preventing repeated overlapping dabs from replacing pickup with their own foreground. Running distinguishes size-linked Automatic blur from Fixed width in document pixels, with physical units resolved at import. Smear does not apply paint density or Running blur. | CSP's separate algorithms, self-intersection behavior, alpha pickup, quality versions and blur kernels. Automatic retains a capped size-linked approximation; Fixed width is the offset of neighboring pickup samples, pending native calibration. |
| Perceptual mixing | Linear-light color interpolation. | CSP's perceptual pigment method; this is explicitly an approximation. |
| Watercolor edge | An alpha-boundary band computed with a tile halo, darkening or varying its color. | CSP boundary width/shape, Standard/Vivid formulas and interaction with grain. |
| Ribbon | Arc-length tiles mapped into connected pressure-varying strips, with flips chosen per material cycle. Static tip angle rotates the registered image before path deformation. | CSP size convention, exact joints, repeat transitions, and interaction between the stored angle and direction input are uncalibrated. Noncardinal wool/knitting imports visibly fail to join correctly. Per-dab angle dynamics and tilt/barrel direction are not yet applied to ribbons. |
| Dual brush | Primary and secondary stroke coverage is combined after independent accumulation. | CSP combination order, source RGB application and exact mode formulas. |
| Post-correction | Endpoint-preserving Gaussian smoothing in arc-length space, after live stabilization. | CSP's correction strength, sharp-corner, speed/scale and Bezier/spline behavior. |
| Percentage start/end | Percentage of final corrected path length, rendered at pen-up. Ending spacing effects also replay with the completed path length. | CSP parameter-specific envelopes and overlap rules. Fade is an independent linear falloff toward the configured minimum. |

Reference-layer anti-overflow and vector-dependent operations remain outside the user's requested raster scope. Broader unavailable brush controls should remain visible in import warnings/metadata rather than being claimed as supported.

Some combinations remain unavailable: ribbons do not run wet pickup, time-based buildup, or separate sprayed strands; their angle changes are limited to the static tip angle. A secondary brush contributes its tip, texture, dynamics and stamp wet pickup, while the primary brush owns the final layer blending, watercolor edge and post-correction. Separate secondary settings for those final stages are not applied. The settings interface and import diagnostics identify these limitations.

## Resource behavior

Decoded materials share a 128 MiB cache budget, including compact RGBA8 image data and encoded-source references. Only sampled pixels are expanded to floats, before interpolation, premultiplication or texture calculations. Each active brush plane retains at most 32 MiB of floating-point tiles before spilling older working tiles to a temporary directory. A dual brush has two such planes. The original-pixel cache is also limited to 32 MiB. Texture and watercolor lookup caches retain at most 32 tile fields each. Dry and wet stamps deposit tile by tile. Wet pickup is snapshotted before deposition in a fixed 64 KiB reservoir per plane; a separate full-footprint staging plane is unnecessary. Completed strokes clear temporary working storage.

Random ribbon repetition retains only the current material choice; one-time shuffled repetition retains at most the material count. Disabled texture-dynamics entries do not allocate per-pixel parameter fields.

Undo snapshots and final document tiles scale with the pixels actually modified; those are necessary document state and are not discarded by the brush working-cache limits. Decoding a large original material still requires a transient full-image allocation. Cache eviction reserves space for the full PNG before a requested mip is reduced. A requested blood brush contains 15 originals totaling 265.7 million pixels: its fitted 640×240 preview improved from 15.37 seconds and a 916 MB native peak (with a repeat failing allocation) to 1.72 seconds and a 403 MB polled process peak. These process figures include more than the retained cache. Pixel-equivalence checks compare the compact sampler and full renderer against the previous float representation for all anti-aliasing levels, material color modes, ribbons and textures.

Input paths are buffered only for post-correction, active percentage tapering, or non-Fade ending effects, and are released at completion. Ordinary brushes and Fade need no additional input buffer. That input list scales with the number of pointer samples; the smoother's resampled workspace is capped at 8193 points. Final replay reuses the same bounded tile caches and publishes one completed raster result.

Offscreen previews and generated sample strokes use explicit deferred publication: `RasterBrushStroke(..., defer_flush=True)` accumulates the same dabs and publishes once at completion. This avoids recomputing live watercolor edges after every input sample when those intermediate images cannot be seen. Interactive canvas strokes keep immediate publication by default. Wet pickup reads immutable pre-stroke pigment and retains its own reservoir, so publication frequency does not change pigment transport. The mode retains the same working-cache limits and adds no input-path buffer.

Live unblurred watercolor edges update only the changed paint region expanded by the edge radius. Neighboring source regions provide the morphology halo across tile boundaries. Completion and blurred post-stroke edges retain the previous full-tile equation. That final work can still take roughly 0.1–0.2 seconds in the examples below. Tiny wet spray particles also retain the fixed 64×64 pickup grid, which is now a larger share of Hazy 1's remaining cost.

## Verification

`tests/test_brush_raster.py` covers opacity accumulation, packet subdivision invariance, stationary airbrush buildup, material color/padding, texture placement and application stage, Color Dodge on translucent coverage, dual masks, alpha erasure and exact undo snapshots, curved/rotated ribbons, material-cycle flips and finite repetition, wet pickup across tile boundaries and transparent gaps, pickup of a second color, dense overlapping paint, bounded reservoir storage, watercolor seams, blend modes over transparency, byte-bounded material caching, forced working-tile spilling/reloading, selection boundaries, dynamic texture strength and paint loading, and anti-aliasing differences.

`tests/test_brush_ribbon_geometry.py` checks preservation of registered padding, finite repetition, exact cardinal-angle pixels against the previous affine mapping, input subdivision, and continuous changes around 45 degrees. The downloaded wool and knitting brushes (asset 2307564) exposed the repeated-corner failure at source angles 26 and 37.1 degrees. Their installed registered-material thumbnails show continuous fabric bands, but may have been supplied by the author; they are visual evidence, not controlled native CSP stroke exports.

The private inspection sheets under `.artifacts/brush-engine/wool-ribbon/` record a rejected row-expansion experiment. `before-after.png` uses identical settings, input gesture, seed and completion replay, and shows that expanding rotated source rows still creates detached blocks while compressing motifs into radiating edges. The experiment was reverted; no arbitrary-angle fix is claimed. `orientation-probe.png` changes only the stored angle: zero degrees connects the intended top/bottom source edges, while 90 degrees exposes their faint left/right borders. Zero is a diagnostic variant, not an import substitution. Raw source scalars are 26.0 and 37.1 with direction flags 67; whether direction input replaces or offsets those values remains unverified.

The source audit follows each embedded material's layer to its original mipmap. That is the only populated raster; the saved render and composite mipmaps are blank. Original pixels exactly match the imported tips, with identity registration scale/rotation and native bounds. No verified material tiling field was recovered. These observations rule out selection of a populated preview in place of the original, but do not establish the native angle or repeat algorithm.

`tests/benchmark_brush_raster.py` measures the real raster path for 81 pointer samples spanning approximately 640 document pixels. On this machine, representative recent runs were about 4 ms median per pen event, 5–7 ms for a textured pencil, 8 ms for wet paint, 4 ms for a chain ribbon, and 8–11 ms for a dual textured pencil. Watercolor completion was about 66–85 ms. These are renderer measurements, not total application input-to-display latency; system load and brush size change them.

`tests/test_brush_correction.py` checks correction metadata/endpoints, default no-buffer behavior, bounded resampling, removal of the live path, exact undo snapshots, preservation of prior transaction snapshots, random/pressure replay, percentage taper resolution, ending-spacing replay against a known completed path length, secondary taper, and preservation of fade during replay.

`tests/test_brush_color.py` checks signed hue wrapping and value limits, constant versus pressure-only color, seeded signed random ranges, per-tip versus per-stroke variation, unchanged material alpha, main/sub/both regions for stamps and ribbons, secondary-brush RGB combination, standard/perceptual sub-color blending, and shifted fresh pigment in wet mixing. Color target behavior follows the [official Color Jitter descriptions](https://help.clip-studio.com/en-us/manual_en/810_subtools/C.htm); exact transfer functions remain independent approximations. The [Ink manual](https://help.clip-studio.com/en-us/manual_en/810_subtools/I.htm) also applies its mixing-space setting to Color Jitter, but does not disclose its numerical method.

`tests/test_brush_wet_transport.py` specifically covers zero fresh-paint density, independent RGB/alpha mixing, partial-alpha pickup, transparent gaps, pressure dynamics, exact undo originals and explicit no-coverage/erase inputs. `docs/csp-downloaded-brush-audit.md` records the downloaded watercolor's original zero-load settings and before/after rendering evidence, plus remaining imported-dynamics gaps.

`tests/test_brush_running_blur.py` checks Automatic/Fixed import and editing, independent pixel/mm widths and DPI re-resolution, dormant settings, preservation of the raw scalar and legacy portable strengths, pressure-driven width, color pickup outside the tip, preview scaling and the downloaded watercolor's Automatic flag. Newly imported Automatic uses the established capped size-linked approximation without reading its dormant fixed slider. The legacy normalized strength is retained internally for unchanged older presets; an explicit new Automatic choice activates strength 1. The interface exposes only the mode and fixed width, matching the native distinction without presenting the approximation's strength as a CSP setting.

`tests/test_brush_material_memory.py` compares selected sampling exactly with the prior full-float pipeline across straight/premultiplied/grayscale material modes, nearest/bilinear interpolation, wrapping, exterior samples and mip levels. Full stroke pixels match byte for byte for all four anti-aliasing settings, stamps and ribbons, and all three tip color modes with textures. It also checks compact read-only storage, absence of full-image float conversion, image-owner lifetime, caller-image detachment and byte-bounded eviction.

`tests/test_brush_deferred_flush.py` compares immediate and deferred final pixels for dry strokes, all three wet modes, dual tips, colored ribbons, live and post-stroke edges, per-stroke dynamic texture, erasing, antialiased selections and correction/taper replay. It verifies that deferred strokes leave the tile store untouched before completion and retain exact undo snapshots.

`tests/test_brush_edge_regions.py` compares every live frame against the complete-tile edge equation for dry, wet, dual and textured paint, fractional and large radii, overlapping/reversing strokes, negative tile boundaries, feathered selections and erasing. It also checks exact undo restoration, tight morphology work regions and preservation of the full-tile post-stroke blur path.

`tests/test_brush_spray.py` checks separate particle/whole-brush angles, single application of stroke direction, inward-facing particles, additive seeded randomness in each direction mode, input-packet invariance, preservation of legacy presets, and primary/secondary serialization. These controls follow the [official Spraying effect descriptions](https://help.clip-studio.com/en-us/manual_en/810_subtools/S.htm). The sampler also keeps stored opacity dynamics dormant for Blend and Running color, as the [Ink manual](https://help.clip-studio.com/en-us/manual_en/810_subtools/I.htm) specifies; Smear and ordinary painting retain opacity dynamics.

Two local inspection sheets were generated under `.artifacts/brush-engine/`: `previews-renderer.png` for original demonstration presets and `installed-previews-renderer.png` for reconstructed installed CSP fixtures. The latter was inspected for preserved color, material detail, connected ribbons, spray and watercolor output. These are Webtoon Maker renderings. They do not contain native CSP comparison strokes, and reconstructed fixtures must not be described as files exported through CSP's user interface.

The user's saved playground was inspected read-only after reported mixing failures. Its wet-paint strokes crossed the blue/yellow patches on the same raster, and saved preset settings still enabled mixing. A repeatable path using those untouched original patches and saved settings reproduced foreground-only Running output. `wet-pickup-before.png` and `wet-pickup-after.png` under `.artifacts/brush-engine/` compare those patches, the saved user strokes, and the controlled path. The reservoir change restores visible pickup and transport without editing the saved project. This verifies the reported functional failure, not native CSP equivalence.
